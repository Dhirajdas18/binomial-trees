European options can be exercised only at expiry, American ones on any day before it.

The drawn tree takes 1 to 12 steps; the convergence chart below runs every step count from 1 to 200.

The tree

How to read it: time runs left to right, from today to expiry, one column per step. Each dot is a share price the tree allows at that step; from every dot the price can move one step up or one step down. Labels show the share price on top and what the option is worth there underneath. At expiry the option is worth what it pays out; every earlier value is the two values after it, weighted and discounted back one step. Orange dots (American only) are where cashing in the option right away is worth more than holding on. Hover over a dot for details.

More steps, closer to Black-Scholes

How to read it: left to right is the number of steps in the tree; height is the option price it gives. The plain tree (blue) zigzags around the Black-Scholes price (grey dashed line) and closes in slowly. Averaging two neighbouring step counts (green) removes most of the zigzag. The Leisen-Reimer tree (purple, odd step counts only) lines its steps up with the strike and is close from a few dozen steps. The view starts at 5 steps; zoom out with the chart’s controls for fewer.
